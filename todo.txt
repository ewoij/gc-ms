## GC-MS Deconvolution Benchmark — Strategy Summary

### Goal
Build a **data generator** that produces realistic synthetic GC-MS intensity matrices with known ground truth, so you can benchmark how well your deconvolution pipeline separates overlapping molecules.

### What the generator produces
An intensity matrix (scans × m/z) containing a mixture of molecules, plus the ground truth (individual components) stored separately for evaluation.

### Generator inputs

**Global parameters:**
- Number of scans (e.g., 120)
- m/z range (e.g., 30–300)
- Baseline level (calibrated from real CDF files — look at Copenhagen Apple Wine or similar)
- Total TIC apex height (also calibrated from real data)

**Per molecule:**
- A mass spectrum (from MoNA library, EI spectra)
- An elution profile shape (extracted from real GC-MS CDF files)
- Apex position (which scan)
- Relative proportion in the mixture (e.g., 70%, 30%)

### How the generator computes the intensity matrix

1. **Normalize** each library spectrum so the ion intensities sum to 1
2. **Split** the TIC apex height by the mixture proportions (e.g., 70% of 2,000,000 = 1,400,000 for molecule A)
3. **Multiply** the normalized spectrum by the molecule's TIC share → gives the apex height of each individual ion
4. **Multiply** each ion's apex height by the elution profile shape over time → gives the full time series per ion
5. **Sum** all molecules together into one combined matrix
6. **Add noise:**
   - Poisson noise (dominant — naturally makes weak signals noisier)
   - Smooth baseline drift (~1,000 counts, slowly varying)
   - Small Gaussian electronic noise

### Where to get the building blocks

**Mass spectra:** Download from MoNA (MassBank of North America) — bulk download the GC-MS/EI subset. Each spectrum is labeled with compound name and structure.

**Elution profile shapes:** Extract from real GC-MS CDF files by picking well-resolved isolated peaks. Good sources:
- Copenhagen datasets (Apple Wine, Camel Cheese, Wine) — CDF format, free
- MassOmics dataset (400 runs, huge variety)
- GNPS/MetaboLights public studies

**Realistic intensity scale:** Open a real CDF file in Python, look at the TIC, note baseline level and typical peak heights. Use those numbers to calibrate your generator parameters.

### Benchmarking your deconvolution

You know the ground truth (each molecule's spectrum, profile, and proportion), so you can measure:
- Does the algorithm recover the correct number of components?
- How closely does each recovered spectrum match the input spectrum? (cosine similarity)
- How closely does each recovered elution profile match the input profile?
- How do results degrade with increasing overlap, increasing noise, or decreasing concentration?

### What to explore later (not now)
- Library matching and compound identification benchmarking (needs standard mixture datasets like FAME-37)
- Quantification benchmarking (needs concentration series with known amounts)
- The **mzrtsim** R package already does something similar and could save you work or serve as a reference implementation
